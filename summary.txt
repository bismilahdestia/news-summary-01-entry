World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Ukraine and Russia agreed a large prisoner-of-war exchange as two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended, with no ceasefire and no agreement on territory, AP reported. US envoy Jared Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian release of the war; the numbers are to be finalised the next day.

Iran's foreign ministry designated Argentina's navy and air force as terrorist organisations, in retaliation for Argentina's designation of the Revolutionary Guards, Reuters reported. Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei told Lebanese television no enriched nuclear material would leave Iran and warned of consequences for any new US strikes.

NATO scrambled jets overnight after at least four drones strayed into Baltic states' airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia, Reuters reported; Lithuania's prime minister called it a difficult night and said no one was hurt.

At the COP31 climate conference in Turkey, UN chief Antonio Guterres told leaders the slow response to climate change had put the world on a path to destruction, Reuters reported.

In Gaza, the season's first storm flooded about 70,000 tents sheltering displaced people, and the US-led Council for Peace called on Israel to let in all necessary humanitarian supplies, BBC reported.

Twelve people died in two fog pile-ups on Bulgaria's Trakia motorway, the prime minister said, BBC reported. In Pakistan, militants ambushed police in Kohat, killing at least 10 officers, Arab News reported. Suspected al-Shabaab fighters killed eight people in an ambush in Mandera, Kenya.

In Italy, at least 300 schools were evacuated after the same bomb alert, presumed a hoax, Reuters reported. Russia's FSB said it arrested two people it called Polish agents in Kaliningrad, Reuters reported. A CloudSek report said hackers linked to Kazakhstan used AI to steal more than 2,000 security documents from Tajikistan, Reuters reported.
